## Setup

- Hands under your shoulders, a little wider than shoulder width, fingers spread.
- One straight line from ears to ankles: squeeze your glutes and brace your stomach.
- Press the floor away so your shoulder blades don't sink between your shoulders.
- Tuck your chin a little and look at the floor just ahead of your hands.

## Cues

- Push the floor away.
- Elbows back at about 45 degrees, not out to the sides.
- Lower your whole body as one piece.
- Touch the floor lightly with your chest.
- At the top, keep pushing until your arms are straight and your shoulder blades spread.

## Common mistakes

- **Sagging hips.** The low back takes the strain. Squeeze your glutes and brace; if the hips still
  drop, raise your hands to an easier step.
- **Flared elbows.** Elbows out at 90 degrees are hard on the shoulders. Bring them to about 45
  degrees and keep your hands only a little wider than your shoulders.
- **A partial range.** Bending the elbows only a little. Lower until your chest nearly touches the
  floor; if you can't with a straight body, use a higher incline rather than shallower reps.
- **A head poke.** The chin reaches for the floor and the neck cranes. Keep a small chin tuck so
  your chest, not your face, gets to the floor first.

## Progressions

Move to the next step when every rep of your sets looks like the setup above.

1. **Wall push-up:** hands on a wall at shoulder height.
2. **Incline push-up:** hands on a counter, then a bench or a low step. The lower your hands, the
   harder it gets.
3. **Knee push-up** (optional): knees on the floor, hips straight, one line from head to knees.
4. **Floor push-up.** In between, practise negatives: lower from the top in three seconds, then
   drop to your knees to come back up.
5. **Slow eccentrics:** full push-ups taking 3–5 seconds to lower.
6. **Further:** feet raised on a step, a pause at the bottom, hands closer together, then one-arm
   work.

**Stop if** you feel sharp pain in your wrists, elbows or shoulders, or numbness or tingling in your
hands. For sore wrists, use a higher incline or hold dumbbell handles so the wrist stays straight.
See a physiotherapist if the pain keeps coming back.
